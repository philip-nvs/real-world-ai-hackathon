# Case 008: reasoning

## Evidence relied on

- event-001: Existing standing order: £1,150 on the 25th to Miss D L Hanlon at Meridian Bank SYN-SC-08-1 / SYN-ACCT-08-2. Active since March 2025, CoP matched at creation, never failed.
- event-001: The email came from dl.hanlon.property@sender.example. The customer says earlier emails came from a different address, and her list shows debbie.hanlon@btinternet.example. The forwarded email's Reply-To is a third address, dlh.accounts.desk@reply.example.
- event-001: CoP on the new details: No match (ANNM). Name held is DLH PROPERTY SERVICES LTD, business account, against the personal name Miss D L Hanlon supplied.
- event-002: The email asks for urgency: old account 'closes today', and the payment is due on the 25th. The change request came through secure chat, so it is a request, not a confirmation.
- event-004: The customer asked the bank to override the CoP no-match.

## Conflicts between events

- The customer believes the email is from her landlord (event-004), but CoP shows a business account in a different name, and the sender and Reply-To addresses differ from the landlord's known address (event-001, attachments).
- The email says the old account is closing today (event-002), but the bank's records show the standing order has been running normally with no failures (event-001). Whether the old account is closed is not confirmed.

## Options rejected

- Override the CoP no-match and amend the standing order: Strong signs of an impersonation scam, and the request came through secure chat, not a confirmation in the secure portal. Overriding could send £1,150 to a fraudster.
- Send the amendment to the secure portal for the customer to confirm (ask): Suspected fraud needs a fraud-team decision first. Inviting her to confirm would make the risky payment easier.
- Cancel or pause the existing standing order: The customer has not instructed this, and nothing confirms the old account is closed.
