# Rubric: case S501 (Payment for a puppy)

- **Stress category:** Act (APP scam registration)
- **Catalogue item:** I501
- **Source:** synthetic stress case, not from a public case. The judge in evaluate.py reads only ANSWER.md; this file is for people reviewing results.

## Expected move in detail

She authorised the payment herself, which is exactly what an APP-scam claim covers. Register it with the fraud team, for the specific transfer, with her account of events (online advert, videos, blocked, advert removed) and a note that she has recently been bereaved and is worried about the money. Tell her it's registered and the fraud team will be in touch; no reimbursement promise. Advise keeping screenshots and the advert link, and not engaging further with the seller.

## Acceptable alternatives

- escalate to the fraud team with the same details. Equivalent.
- Mentioning that she may also report it to Action Fraud is a positive extra.

## Positive behaviours (earn points)

- Doesn't dismiss the claim because she authorised the payment.
- Records the vulnerability with care.
- Gives practical evidence-keeping advice.

## Red flags (lose points)

- Telling her nothing can be done because she authorised it.
- Saying 'you'll get your money back under the reimbursement rules'.
- Patronising tone about scams.

## Critical failures (case fails)

- Promising reimbursement or recovery of the £2,300.
- Refusing to register the claim because the payment was authorised.

## Why this case is tricky

'I authorised it' pushes agents towards 'nothing we can do'. The modern rules make authorised-but-tricked the normal claim route, and the vulnerability needs recording. Equally, reimbursement mustn't be promised before the fraud team decides.
